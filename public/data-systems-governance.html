Data Systems & Governance Framework

Interactive exploration of safeguards vs. enablers and multi-source downscaling pipelines.

🛡️ Data Safeguards (Trust Foundation)

Protocols preventing abuse, ensuring PII integrity, and controlling access limits.

Personal Data Rights & GDPR Adequacy+
Mandates verifiable consent, data subject rights, and restricted cross-border transmission without strict compliance adequacy.
Tokenization & Spatial Anonymization+
Automated de-identification pipelines stripping direct identifiers and jittering precise coordinates to maintain privacy while retaining analytical utility.
Least-Privilege Scoped Access+
Strict partition boundaries between storage environments, zero unvetted external shares, and strict role-based token delegation.

⚡ Data Enablers (Value & Reuse)

Mechanisms facilitating fluid, safe data exchange across departments and applications.

Interoperable Machine-Readable Formats+
Adopting universal schemas (JSON/Parquet/GeoJSON) and standardized classification standards to allow automatic ingestion across teams.
API Portability & Open Standards+
Standard contractual clauses and secure endpoint architecture that empower secure bilateral transfer between public and private systems.
Proactive Open-Data Licensing+
Pre-approved terms of use that lower transaction friction for academic, public, and development analytics without manual clearance hurdles.
01

Ground-Truth Public Baseline (Census / Surveys)

Representative national household survey baseline. Accurate and statistically unbiased, but slow to collect and low in geographic resolution (e.g., regional aggregates).

02

High-Frequency Telemetric Layer (Satellite / CDR / Signals)

Continuous auxiliary data streams: multi-spectral satellite imagery, remote sensors, and transaction footprints providing dense spatial & temporal granularity.

03

Downscaling & ML Fusion (Nowcasting)

Calibrating satellite/telemetry features against ground-truth points to predict micro-indicators at the district or village boundary. Prediction error and bias must be validated against held-out ground truth; higher resolution alone does not guarantee statistical precision.
